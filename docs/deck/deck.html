Agents can pay bills now. Nobody can safely let them.

A wallet is all or nothingGive an agent wallet credentials and it can pay anyone, any amount. Take them away and it can't do the job.
Caps are yes or noSpend limits and allowlists either let a payment through or fail it. The payments that matter most, a new vendor or a bigger invoice, need a person in the loop.
The fraud is in the details"Our wallet changed, pay this address." The name matches; the address doesn't. Business email compromise works the same way on agents.

Finance teams need three things before they let an agent touch a treasury: a mandate the agent can't exceed, a person who signs the exceptions, and a record they can check without trusting the agent.

02

Twokey decides every payment, in one of four lanes.

✓ Inside the mandate

Pays on its own through Circle Wallets on Arc, in seconds, with the tx hash on the record.

✋ Needs you

New payee or over a cap: waits for a hold, sealed with SHA-256 over amount, address, chain, token, memo and expiry.

↻ Sending

Right before paying, the executor re-classifies and re-verifies the seal. One changed cent voids it.

⊘ Refused

A pinned name at a new address, over the hard max, or short on USDC for amount + gas: blocked before any Circle call.

The agent holds only a Twokey key. It can ask; it can never approve, and it never sees a wallet id. Approvals happen in the console or on Telegram.
